Double-Stops & Triads

Hear two notes. Add one more. Discover the chord inside the shape.

New to double-stops? Start here

A double-stop is two notes played at the same time. Thirds, fourths, fifths and sixths all make useful double-stops. Guitarists use them to harmonize melodies, decorate chords and connect chord shapes.

A triad has three distinct note names, usually a root, third and fifth. The root names the chord; the third helps establish major or minor quality; the fifth completes its shape. Repeating a note in another octave does not add a new chord tone.

TriadFormulaExample
Major1–3–5E–G♯–B
Minor1–♭3–5F♯–A–C♯
Diminished1–♭3–♭5D♯–F♯–A
Augmented1–3–♯5E–G♯–B♯

Major stacks a major third then a minor third; minor reverses that order. Diminished stacks two minor thirds; augmented stacks two major thirds. B♯ sounds like C, but its spelling shows that it is E’s raised fifth. The augmented example is outside E major.

The interval between two notes does not determine the whole chord: G♯–B is a minor third inside E major, where those notes are the third and fifth.

3 · Let the Open Strings Ring

Open strings sustain while your fretting hand moves. E, A and B belong to E major, but belonging to the key does not make every note stable over every chord. D and G are chromatic here.

Add a chord first, then try an available open string. Fretted strings cannot also ring open. A slash names the lowest sounding note: E/G♯ is E major with G♯ in the bass.

E–G♯–B: complete major triad. A–C♯–E–B: Aadd9, a triad plus a ninth. E–A–B: Esus4, with the fourth replacing the third. Keeping a third and adding a fourth creates tension; it does not make a sus4 chord.

A little tension, then home

Try E–A at D2/G2: a perfect fourth. Add B for Esus4, then move A down one fret to G♯ to resolve to E major. The fourth alone is neither major nor minor.

Why the shapes change

A third skips one letter: E–G♯, F♯–A, and so on. A major third spans four semitones; a minor third spans three. One fret is one semitone. Stay within E major and the order is major, minor, minor, major, major, minor, minor, major.

On D and G, the G-string note is one fret nearer the nut for a major third and two frets nearer for a minor third. These two shapes move along this string pair. Shapes crossing G and B need an adjustment because those open strings are tuned differently.

These are two-note fragments. A triad has three distinct notes. Adding a seventh makes a seventh chord; an added ninth supplies another color. “No fifth” identifies an omitted note. An extension adds a note; a suspension replaces the third. Keeping the third and adding an 11th is not a sus4 chord.
