# Double-Stops & Triads

Open **Learn → Double-Stops & Triads**. This extends the existing E-major-thirds lesson in place. The fretboard explorer, triad visualizer, modes, game, tuner and metronome remain independent. No microphone is needed.

## A short teaching path

1. **Hear two notes.** Select E–G♯ near the nut, by either note, the dashed oval, or the harmony menu. Pluck D2 and G1 together with P + I. A major third spans four semitones; a minor third spans three. A double-stop is a simultaneous two-note interval, not automatically a complete chord.
2. **Build a chord.** Tap **Add B → E**. Open B illuminates, completes 1–3–5, and sounds with the original pair. Switch between pitch names and chord-tone labels. “Back to two notes” removes the added notes. Choose an alternative recipe where available.
3. **Explore the harmony.** Open **Let the Open Strings Ring**. Add or remove an available open string and watch the sounding notes, chord name, bass, role labels and TAB update together. Try A → Aadd9, E/G♯ → E with low E, or F♯m → F♯m7 with high E. Compare D♯–F♯ completed with B versus A. Try E–A → Esus4, then hear A resolve to G♯.
4. **Make music.** The first TAB example pinches the pair on beat 1, lets it ring, and adds the missing notes separately from beat 2. The second arpeggiates only the selected voicing. Add low E to the first E triad for alternating low-E/D-string-E bass; add high E for the full P-I-M-A roll. Follow any of the three progressions, then try the five guided challenges.

The first exercise asks the learner to find E–G♯. The next three ask for missing fifths (B, C♯, E). The last asks why the minor third G♯–B can belong to E major. Wrong answers offer a retry; correct answers demonstrate the completed harmony. There is no score or time pressure.

## Guitar conventions and visual language

- Low E at left, high e at right, headstock at top; fret numbers run toward the body. Notes sit between fret wires. Markers are at 3, 5, 7, 9 and double 12. The string/open-note strip stays visible while the frets scroll; the actual nut belongs to the beginning of the fretboard and scrolls away.
- Pitch labels are not fretting-finger numbers. Chord-tone mode uses 1 (R), 3, ♭3, 5, ♭5, ♭7, with 4 and 9 for the suspension and ninth. Conventional chord diagrams may instead show fretting fingers 1–4.
- D-string scale notes are blue, G-string harmonies cream, added notes gold. Green/purple outlines describe **interval quality**. Chord names/buttons independently use green major, purple minor, orange dominant seventh, gray diminished. Selected pairs brighten; other pairs remain context, not instructions to strum every displayed note.
- The complete dashed oval and either note are selectable with pointer, Enter or Space. SVG buttons have note/fret accessible names, visible focus, and larger invisible hit areas. Respect reduced-motion preferences for the added-note pulse.
- The open row shows the sounding pitch (or chord role), X for skipped strings, and blank for fretted strings. A dotted gold question mark previews the open note needed to complete the chord.
- Standard six-line TAB has high e at top, low E at bottom, 0 for open strings, and eight evenly spaced counts in 4/4. Aligned numbers connected vertically mean a simultaneous pinch. Separate columns are successive attacks; a dash in the picking row means no fresh pluck. Notes sustain within the bar.
- Picking letters use P thumb on E/A/D, I on G, M on B, A on high e. Pinky anchoring is optional. These are introductory arpeggios, not a claim to teach a strict banjo roll or Travis pattern.

## Important musical distinctions

- A triad contains three distinct pitch classes, built from root/third/fifth. Major 1–3–5; minor 1–♭3–5; diminished 1–♭3–♭5; augmented 1–3–♯5. The introductory E augmented example correctly spells B♯ (enharmonic C) and identifies it as outside the key.
- G♯–B and D♯–F♯ are minor thirds that can be the third/fifth of **major** chords E and B. A pair's interval does not determine every chord it can belong to.
- E, A and B are in E major but are not universally stable. Open D/G are chromatic here; those strings are also occupied by the selected pair. One string cannot sound both open and fretted simultaneously.
- Open additions are explicit, never silently carried into a different recipe. Available additions identify the new harmony. Unsupported tense combinations are explained rather than assigned a false major/minor name.
- B7 with open A is B7/A. D♯ diminished with low open A is D♯dim/A. C♯m7 with open B and no fifth is C♯m7/B (no fifth). High E added to F♯–A gives F♯m7 (no fifth); high E added to the complete F♯ minor triad gives F♯m7. Low E instead puts E in the bass.
- Aadd9 contains the third and ninth with no seventh; it is not A9. E–A–B is Esus4 because the fourth replaces the third. Adding a fourth while retaining the third does not create a sus4 chord.
- B-string frets 7 and 9 supply the same F♯/G♯ pitches as high-e frets 2 and 4, with much smaller reaches from the high-neck double-stops. Practical fretted voicings span no more than two frets. The original theoretical recipes and stretch warnings remain available.

## Implementation and verification

`js/thirds.js` shares `GuitarMusic.tuning`, retains the original recipes/APIs, and adds a pure lesson-state model. The model calculates exact sounding MIDI pitches, pitch sets, note roles, inversions, available open additions, explanations and TAB events. It recognizes the lesson's supported chord formulas rather than acting as a universal chord-name guesser.

`js/thirds-lesson.js` renders the existing lesson and handles its controls. The same selected state drives diagram, text, TAB and playback. It reuses `GuitarAudio.ensureContext` and `GuitarAudio.pluck`, resumes after user gestures, and owns a cancellable gain/timer session. Changing notes, leaving the lesson, opening Help, backgrounding, or pressing Stop ends lesson sound. The implementation adds no dependencies, microphone capture or scoring changes. It remains static and compatible with GitHub Pages.

Run `node --test tests/*.test.cjs` (108 passing tests at completion). `tests/thirds.test.cjs` independently checks all eight MIDI pairs, exact chord pitch sets/basses/omissions, practical hand spans, one pitch per string, completion, roles, open additions, suspended resolution and timed picking events. `tests/thirds-ui.test.cjs` executes the real controller against a small DOM/audio boundary to check selection → build → labels → TAB → audio synchronization, Enter/Space, reset, open bass changes, filtered progressions and all guided challenges.

Browser checks covered desktop, portrait 390 × 844 and landscape 844 × 390: all eight outline selections, keyboard activation, adding notes, role labels, open E bass, diminished alternatives, guided retries/completion, B7/A progression, Esus4 resolution, and TAB playback after a gesture. No horizontal page overflow was observed; TAB has its own horizontal scroll area. Automated checks cover existing modes, tuner, metronome, game timing/scoring, microphone lifecycle and audio behavior.

**Limitations:** These responsive checks use Chromium phone-sized frames, not physical iOS Safari. Actual iPhone audio output, touch ergonomics and Safari behavior still need device testing. Playback is a synthesized preview. The lesson reuses fingerpicking conventions/audio, but does not yet register exercises in the microphone-scored practice game.
